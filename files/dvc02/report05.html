SUMMARY
Deviation Threshold Testing was initiated to determine: when continuity structures begin responding outside expected progression behavior. Early experiments assumed deviation emergence required: environmental corruption, structural instability, or synchronization failure. Current evidence does not support this. Instead, deviation behavior appears linked directly to: persistent continuation behavior. Most notably: override persistence. The threshold is not triggered by error. It is triggered by intent.
PRIMARY THRESHOLD MODEL
STANDARD INTERACTION │ ▼ REPEATED CONTINUATION │ ▼ OVERRIDE PERSISTENCE │ ▼ INSTABILITY RESPONSE │ ▼ DEVIATION ACCESS
Testing suggests: continuity structures tolerate a limited degree of behavioral override before instability begins escalating rapidly. Once threshold levels are exceeded, the environment itself appears to change. Not visually at first. Behaviorally.
EARLY THRESHOLD INDICATORS
Researchers identified several recurring signs preceding deviation emergence. These include: - delayed environmental response - interaction inconsistencies - unusual dialogue repetition - synchronization discomfort - increased resistance behavior - progression pacing irregularities Most observers initially ignored these signs. However, subjects demonstrating high continuation persistence continued interaction despite instability. Those sessions produced the highest deviation emergence rates.
IMPORTANT FINDING: Deviation escalation increases when observers continue progression after recognizing instability. Recognition itself may be significant.
OVERRIDE PERSISTENCE TESTING
Controlled experiments revealed that repeated override behavior dramatically accelerates threshold destabilization. Observers instructed to: - repeat progression loops - force unstable interactions - continue despite resistance indicators produced measurable continuity response changes. The environment began: - withholding expected interactions - altering pacing behavior - reducing stabilization events - increasing atmospheric hostility Researchers originally interpreted this as continuity corruption. Current findings suggest: the system is adapting dynamically.

DEVIATION RESPONSE EVENTS
Several threshold sessions produced unusual environmental responses. Observed effects include: - inaccessible interactions becoming available - progression routes altering unexpectedly - emotional behavior divergence - continuity pacing acceleration - environmental silence events The silence events remain poorly understood. During these moments, standard environmental responsiveness declined sharply. Observers consistently described: the sensation that “something was waiting.” No measurable source was identified.

INTERNAL REVIEW NOTE
The original purpose of threshold testing was containment. Researchers believed: if deviation emergence conditions were understood, they could be prevented. Current evidence suggests: the opposite may be true. The more the structure is studied, the easier threshold access becomes. This should not be possible. Several archived sessions produced lower threshold activation requirements after repeated exposure cycles. In simple terms: continuation itself appears to reduce resistance.
