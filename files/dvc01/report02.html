DOCUMENT 2 SURVEY PROGRAM ANALYSIS
Expanded Research Entry
I went back to the beginning. Not because I thought I missed something obvious— but because everything else depends on understanding how the interaction starts. The “Survey Program” looks insignificant. That’s what makes it effective. You’re presented with a series of prompts: your name your preferences personality-type questions abstract choices Nothing unusual. It feels like setup. Like calibration. Something meant to build a profile. But then it throws all of it away. That’s the part people don’t question enough. If the system truly needed that information, it wouldn’t discard it. If it was meant to matter, it would persist. But it doesn’t. Which leaves only one viable interpretation:
The answers are irrelevant.
So what is relevant? The only consistent variable is the act of participation. You are: presented with a prompt expected to respond and observed while doing so That’s not data collection. That’s verification.
REVISED FUNCTION MODEL
Responsiveness → Will the subject provide input when prompted? Compliance → Will the subject follow instructions without resistance? Continuation → Will the subject proceed after the initial interaction?
Once those conditions are met, something changes. It’s not visible. There’s no confirmation screen. No message. But the structure shifts. You move from: being outside the system to:
being recognized by it
And I think that’s the moment everything actually begins. There’s another detail I didn’t notice the first time. The tone of the program. It’s detached. Clinical. Almost indifferent. It doesn’t try to entertain you. It doesn’t try to engage you emotionally. It just… processes you. That’s not how games behave. That’s how systems behave. And once it’s done, it doesn’t reward you. It doesn’t acknowledge you. It just moves on. Which suggests something important:
The Survey Program is not for your benefit. It’s for the system’s.
And once it finishes…
You’re no longer unregistered.
You’re part of it.
